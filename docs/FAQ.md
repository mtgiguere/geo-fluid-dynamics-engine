# Questions you will get

Ready answers to the questions people ask when they see the wave player
(`web/public/waves.html`) or hear the talk. Each answer has a short version
(one breath) and a longer one. The numbers come from `docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md`
(fourth to ninth looks).

---

## "How do you know the neighbours did it, and not something else?"

People ask this in many forms: Santa Claus, Kermit the Frog, the weather,
a TV ad, "they're just similar people". It is always the same question:
**could some other cause explain the pattern?** The answer works for any
cause they name.

**Short version:**

> We don't claim to know what changed each county's mind. We test every
> kind of outside cause we can measure, and the pattern survives. What it
> tells you holds whatever the cause: where an idea caught on last month
> is where it shows up next. That is what you plan around.

**Longer version.** An outside cause has to behave in one of a few ways,
and each one leaves a different fingerprint:

| If the cause... | it would look like... | What we see |
|---|---|---|
| **Is luck** | the same number of hand-offs if you shuffled the dates around the state | Solar: more hand-offs than chance (New York: 80 against 49) |
| **Hits everyone at once** (national news, a tax credit, a debate, a holiday) | every county moving in the same week | The model takes out each state's week-by-week rhythm first; the neighbour effect is what is left over |
| **Is that neighbours are alike** (income, politics, sunshine) | the counties that go *after* you predicting your timing as well as those that went *before* | Before beats after: solar 2.4-3x, laws ~1.6x, baby names ~10x, a new candidate's donors ~1.3x |
| **Is local TV covering several counties** | the pattern stopping at the edge of a media market | It crosses metro lines (1.3x) |
| **Travels county by county, in order** (a salesman, a field office opening one town at a time) | exactly what we see | **Cannot be told apart with this data.** See below |

**The one we can't rule out, and why it doesn't hurt you.** If something
travels county by county in order (an installer expanding its territory, a
campaign opening offices outward), it leaves the same footprint as
neighbours influencing each other. Two ways to tell them apart:
- **Data on the traveller:** installer records, field-office dates.
- **An experiment:** work some counties and leave matched ones alone, then
  watch whether the neighbours of the worked counties catch on sooner.
  That is the first client engagement we propose.

For planning, it does not matter which it is. Either way, the counties next
to the ones that just caught on are where it shows up next.

**Say:** "Neighbours went first more often than chance, and more than
look-alikes explain."
**Don't say:** "Neighbours caused it." That is a claim only an experiment
can earn.

## "I watched the map and couldn't see a wave for the candidates."

**Short version:**

> Right. It is there in the numbers but too faint to see. That is why you
> need someone to measure it.

**Longer:** Rooftop solar beats chance clearly enough to see (New York). A
new candidate's donor base spreads too (Buttigieg 1.6x in the model), but
on a map the hand-offs look like chance. A faint signal over thousands of
counties is real and usable, but only by measuring it, not by eyeballing.

## "Isn't this just news? Everyone heard about it at the same time."

**Short version:**

> Sometimes, yes, and we can tell you when. A news shock lands everywhere
> on the same day; a slow idea travels.

**Longer:** After the Dobbs ruling, 735 of 797 counties' new-donor surge
fell on the same day; on Biden's launch week, nearly every county caught on
at once. Those are broadcasts, and the model says so: hand-offs at chance.
Waves belong to slow ideas: a product people see, a name people hear, a
policy states copy, a little-known candidate. The tool tells you which kind
you are dealing with, and the plan differs:
- **Broadcast:** have the ask ready everywhere on day one.
- **Wave:** follow it, one county ahead.

---

## "Why not just use polls?"

**Short version:**

> Polls tell you where people stand today. This tells you where an idea is
> headed next, from things people actually did, dated to the day.

## "Couldn't anyone do this? The code is public."

**Short version:**

> Yes, and that is on purpose: you can check every number. What takes
> skill is setting it up for your idea.

**Longer:** Each idea travels on its own roads (laws and baby names share
few). The settings that make a wave visible differ by idea: the mark, the
window, the data source. Some patterns that look like a wave are not (a
ripple out of a city is city first, countryside later, not neighbours).
Getting those calls right is the work.
