Geo-Fluid Dynamics Engine · Prescriptive Layer

Don't show me the map.
Tell me where to drive.

Most civic tools stop at a colored map. This one reads the same public election data and hands a volunteer in Kansas City a weekend plan: which counties to canvass, in what order, and which to skip — each with the reason in plain English.

Act 1 · What everyone already has

A map tells you what happened.

Here's how Kansas voted on abortion rights in August 2022 — every county, shaded by the result. It's accurate. It's even pretty. And if you're an organizer with a tank of gas and a free Saturday, it's a dead end. A map describes the past. It doesn't tell you where to stand on Saturday morning.

Descriptive analytics answer “what is.” An organizer needs to know “what do I do.” That's the gap.

Act 2 · The tell

Some red counties are persuadable. The data says which.

Take Miami County, just south of the city. Only about 30% of it voted Democratic for president — solidly Republican turf. Yet a majority voted to protect abortion rights. That gap, between how a place votes its party and how it votes its conscience, is what we call dissonance. A high-dissonance county is a False Bastion: red on the surface, movable underneath.

That's the signal worth driving toward — not the deepest-red county (unmovable) and not your own backyard (already with you), but the places quietly voting ahead of their party.

The proof it's predictive
r = 0.62

In Ohio, the same 88 counties voted on two different questions the same day — abortion and cannabis. The counties that broke from their party on one broke on the other too (correlation 0.62, holding partisanship constant). Persuadability is a stable trait of a place — not a one-issue fluke. That makes this a prediction, not a coincidence.

A word on “riding the wave”

We tested whether political change ripples county-to-county like a stone dropped in a pond.

In presidential voting, it mostly doesn't — it's one national tide hitting everywhere at once, not a wavefront you can stand in front of. We have the analysis that shows it. Knowing that is why this tool targets persuadable places instead of phantom waves. Good prediction starts with refusing to predict what you can't.

Every number here is computed by tested code from public records — the Kansas Secretary of State's certified canvass and county presidential returns. The classifications, the distances, the ranking: all under test, no hand-tuning.
