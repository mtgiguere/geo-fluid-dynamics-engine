Geo-Fluid Dynamics Engine · wave player (draft)

Watch an idea travel

Pick an idea and press play. Each county darkens as the idea fills it in. The orange flash is the moment a county crosses the mark (a set share of its own final total): the edge of the wave.

Each county is coloured by the share of its own final total reached so far, so a big county and a small one are read by the shape of their arrival, not their size. The line under the map is the share of counties past the mark: a wave draws an S-curve over months, a broadcast draws a cliff.

"Learning ratio": in the hazard model, how much more a county is pulled by neighbours who went in the last few weeks than by neighbours who go in the next few (which it could not have learned from); 95% ranges from resampling states. 1 means no neighbour-to-neighbour spread. Details: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md.

Arrows (when zoomed to a state): when a county crosses the mark, an arrow comes from each neighbour that crossed in the few frames before it. They draw the hand-offs the hazard model counts. An arrow is not proof the idea crossed that border; the learning ratio is the test of that. The "clearest state" for each idea is the one where hand-offs most clearly beat chance. "By chance alone": the arrows you would expect if each state's crossing times were shuffled among its counties (computed exactly, no random draws). A broadcast lands near 1×. Candidates' waves are real in the model (learning ratio up to 1.6) but stay near 1× here: too small to see by eye.

Sources: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Tracking the Sun (solar); FEC, ActBlue's raw filings (donors, counted by ZIP and day; no names kept); Census ZIP-county file. Alaska, Hawaii and Puerto Rico not shown.
